What it is

One question, four ways of answering it

Symbolic Systems (often shortened to “SymSys”) asks how meaning can live in symbols: words in a sentence, neurons in a brain, bits in a program. Founded at Stanford in 1986, the program treats the human mind and the computer as two kinds of symbol-processing systems and studies them side by side.

Every student completes the same Core, which covers four breadth areas, then goes deep through a five-course concentration such as Artificial Intelligence, Human-Computer Interaction, Neurosciences, or Natural Language. The major ends with hands-on experiential requirements: an advanced small seminar, a capstone practicum, an integrative course, and a public presentation.

Fine print

Major policies

Core grades

C- or better (or a no-option S grade). Every Core course must be 3+ units.

Concentration grades

D- or better. CR grades from letter-grade-optional courses don't count.

No double counting

Each course meets only one requirement, except listed overlaps such as the small seminar with the concentration.
